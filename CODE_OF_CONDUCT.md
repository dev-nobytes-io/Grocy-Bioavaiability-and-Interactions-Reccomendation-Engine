# Code of conduct

This project argues about evidence that affects people's health. Disagreement is expected. These rules keep it productive.

## Expected

- **Argue with evidence, not with people.** Cite the source. Say what it shows and what it does not.
- **Change your mind when the evidence does.** Say so plainly when you do.
- **Be direct and civil.** Critique the work. Leave out comments about the person.
- **Disclose conflicts of interest** when you propose or review a rule.
- **Respect people's time.** Read the linked documents before asking a question they answer.

## Not accepted

- Harassment, threats, insults, or personal attacks.
- Discrimination or slurs of any kind.
- Posting anyone's private information, including their health data, without their consent.
- Giving individual medical advice in project spaces. Point the person to a clinician instead.
- Deliberately misrepresenting a source.
- Promoting a product you are paid to promote without saying so.

## Scope

These rules apply in all project spaces: issues, pull requests, discussions, and any chat or forum the project runs. They also apply when you represent the project elsewhere.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove content, issue a warning, or ban someone temporarily or permanently. The response fits the behaviour. A first minor lapse usually gets a quiet word. Deliberate harassment gets a ban.

Report a problem privately to the maintainers through [GitHub's report content feature](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam) or a [private security advisory](https://github.com/dev-nobytes-io/Grocy-Bioavaiability-and-Interactions-Reccomendation-Engine/security/advisories/new) if the report involves personal data. Maintainers will keep the reporter's identity private.

A maintainer who is the subject of a report does not take part in handling it.
